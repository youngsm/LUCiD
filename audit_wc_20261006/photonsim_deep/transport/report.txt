PhotonSim charged and hadronic transport review

Four physical problems were reproduced in the pinned PhotonSim v1.0.2
source using Geant4 11.3.0. The largest new problem is the capture target
chosen for negative pions in water. Production files were not changed.

1. Negative pions capture on hydrogen far too often in water.

Geant4's generic element selector assigns hydrogen about 30.9% of stopped
negative-pion captures in H2O. The measured final nuclear-capture fraction
on hydrogen is 0.445 +/- 0.024%. The selected target passes unchanged through
the stopping process and Bertini cascade; there is no subsequent transfer
that repairs this choice.

In 3,000 cold-pion events, the baseline selected hydrogen 880 times and
created 880 capture pi0 daughters. A material- and species-specific selector
using the measured probability selected hydrogen 19 times. Mean emitted
Cherenkov light fell from 9,435 +/- 243 to 1,080 +/- 46 photons.

The effect remains large at an energy used in the gun production config:
for 1,000 injected monoenergetic 200 MeV pi- events per variant, the baseline
produced 162 stopped-capture pi0
showers, versus two with the corrected selector. Mean emitted light fell
from 24,808 +/- 601 to 18,604 +/- 515 photons, approximately 25%. This is
not a measured fractional effect for the full GENIE distribution or PMT PE.

Minimal fix: WaterPionElementSelector.hh samples the measured H/O target
probability and retains Geant4's natural-isotope selection. Its PDG and
material guards are essential because the Bertini stopping process is
shared with negative kaons and hyperons. The runtime variant passed the
independent target-fraction oracle. This fixes target choice only; item 4
describes remaining final-state errors.

Physical reference: Berridge et al., Physical Review A 75, 034501 (2007),
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.75.034501 . The reported fraction already
includes the Panofsky correction; it is not an initial atomic-capture rate.

2. Negative muons have the wrong capture/decay mixture in water.

The same generic selector leaves roughly 31% of stopped muons assigned to
hydrogen. In real water, muonic hydrogen transfers to oxygen much faster
than the muon disappears. This changes whether a Michel electron or nuclear
capture products are generated.

For 10,000 mu- injected with small positive kinetic energy and allowed to
stop, the baseline capture fraction was 12.86 +/- 0.335%, and the mean
disappearance delay was 1,926.5 +/- 19.4 ns. Water measurements give
18.4 +/- 0.1% and 1,795.4 +/- 2.0 ns. An oxygen-selector variant gave
18.17 +/- 0.386% and 1,814.9 +/- 18.1 ns, consistent with both measurements.
The fraction with a tracked Michel daughter above 1 MeV before the accepted
10 us cut changed from
86.49% to 81.53%; this is not detector or tagging efficiency. Separate
200 MeV samples also failed the baseline oracle
and passed after the target correction. The positive-muon control passed.

Minimal fix: attach WaterMuonElementSelector.hh to G4MuonMinusCapture using
SetElementSelector. This preserves isotope sampling and represents rapid
transfer through the final oxygen target. It does not model microscopic
atomic-transfer timing and cascade details. Switching to
G4MuonMinusAtomicCapture alone does not fix the target choice.

Physical reference: Super-Kamiokande, Physical Review D 110, 082008 (2024),
Transfer discussion in Section III B, capture fraction on printed page 12,
and lifetime in Table XII on printed page 18:
https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/340602/1/340602.pdf .

The observer classifies direct daughters as decay-in-orbit or nuclear
capture before stacking cuts, using their creator-model IDs. It records
their birth times; Geant4 kills the muon parent at the stopping time while
assigning delayed times to its daughters. Late rejected daughters are not
misclassified as captures. The 200 MeV lifetime/capture comparison uses
only muons that stopped, excluding rare decays in flight.

3. Photonuclear, electronuclear, and muon-nuclear processes are omitted.

The actual process managers have no electronNuclear, positronNuclear, or
muonNuclear process; the combined gamma process has a null nuclear process.
PhotonSim omits G4EmExtraPhysics, which the complete Geant4 QGSP_BERT
reference list registers. Adding that constructor restores the processes.

For 2,000 gamma events at 25 MeV, the added constructor produced 75
photonuclear interactions. Mean emitted light changed from
5,287.46 +/- 7.55 to 5,109.70 +/- 21.47 photons, a 3.36% reduction at
7.81 approximate standard errors. It restored a near-zero-light tail that
was entirely absent from the baseline sample.

At 2 GeV, photonuclear interactions appeared in 30.75% of gamma showers
and 28.25% of electron showers. Neutron-producing event fractions became
4.75% and 5.75%, versus zero in their respective baseline samples. Mean
light changed by approximately -0.77% for both species. The main GeV
consequence is missing secondary topology and low-light tails, not a huge
universal light-scale error. For 1,000 2 GeV mu+ events, the light difference
was statistically negligible; no direct muon-nuclear interaction occurred.

Minimal fix: include G4EmExtraPhysics.hh and register
new G4EmExtraPhysics(0). See minimal_em_extra_fix.diff. Actual process
presence, restored nuclear interactions, and the changed gamma tail were
checked in the executed regression.

4. Radiative pion-capture final states are missing even after target repair.

All 880 baseline hydrogen captures yielded pi0 n. The pinned Bertini
two-body channel table contains no gamma n final state. The measured
Panofsky ratio is 1.546, implying approximately 39.3% electromagnetic
capture-family branching on hydrogen. That family includes a small internal
conversion channel; assigning all of it to real gamma n is a leading-channel
approximation. After correcting target probabilities, the missing family
affects roughly 0.175% of all water pion stops.

The oxygen omission is larger: the corrected sample contains 2,981 oxygen
captures and zero immediate capture photons above 10 MeV. Independently
measured hard components alone have branching fractions of
0.15 +/- 0.03% near 128.1 MeV and 0.25 +/- 0.06% around 117-121 MeV. They
predict about 12 photons in this sample, before adding the larger continuum.
Their absolute-rate errors share normalization uncertainty. Using the sum
of those errors as a conservative bound, reducing the combined branching
by three such bounds leaves approximately 2.1% probability to observe zero.
The central expectation and complete absence of the model channel are the
main evidence; no independent-error assumption is made.

The measured total oxygen radiative branch is 2.24 +/- 0.48%, but this
includes an extrapolation below 50 MeV; it must not be described as a
directly measured above-threshold branching fraction. The hard-line check
avoids that ambiguity. See test_radiative_oracle.py and radiative_results.json.

A minimal hydrogen correction can use a dedicated pi- stopping process
with a custom hadronic interaction: select gamma n or pi0 n with the
measured branching, and generate an energy-conserving two-body final state.
For oxygen, use measured gamma lines/continuum with a consistent recoiling
residual nucleus and de-excitation, for example gamma plus excited 16N.
Adding a gamma to the existing absorption event would violate its energy
budget. Neither radiative model was implemented or claimed validated here.

Primary references and exact implementation hooks are documented in
reference/pion_radiative_oracles.md. The oxygen author manuscript is
https://escholarship.org/content/qt5835t658/qt5835t658.pdf , printed page 24.

Reproduction, provenance, and remaining limits

From /sdf/group/neutrino/youngsam/sim/lucid-data/LUCID/audit_wc_20261006/photonsim_deep/transport,
submit run_transport.sbatch, run_capture.sbatch, run_final_checks.sbatch, and
run_radiative_check.sbatch using sbatch. The substantive simulations completed on milano
jobs 39971259 and 39971585; final assertions and fix-snippet compilation
passed on milano job 39972145. The radiative statistical check is job
39972650, rerun successfully as 39973045 after conservatively handling
correlated measurement errors. Job 39973141 passed the final radiative
oracle and required every transport fixture to have its expected row count
and finite values. No tests, imports, numerical scripts, or builds ran on
login CPUs.

The isolated harness retains the original charged/hadronic actions and
counts Cherenkov secondaries, then kills optical tracks to avoid transport
and ROOT-storage cost. This changes random-number consumption. Comparisons
are ensemble comparisons, not claims of paired event identity. Photon
counts are emitted source light, not detected PMT photoelectrons.

Minimal assertion examples are in test_new_findings.py. The water target
test can be reduced to loading the capture CSV, dividing the hydrogen
capture count by all stopped-pion captures, and checking agreement with
0.00445. Baseline failure and isolated-fix success are both explicitly
verified. Full observations are in capture_results.json, em_results.json,
results.json, test_results.json, and radiative_results.json.

Actual processes were inspected for gamma, e-/e+, mu-/mu+, proton, neutron,
pi-/pi+, K-/K+/K0L, and alpha. Hadron elastic/inelastic interactions,
negative-hadron stopping, neutron capture, ion physics, and ordinary decays
are present. Proton, kaon, and neutron samples were also exercised.
Muon stopping ranges at 200, 400, 1,000, and 2,000 MeV agreed with independent
PDG water CSDA values within 0.65%; no large charged-range scale error was
found. These checks do not establish the physical accuracy of every
hadronic differential final state. Prompt nuclear de-excitation models,
fine neutron response, and GENIE primary polarization remain separate
validation topics. Accepted late-light and detector approximations were
not counted as new bugs.
