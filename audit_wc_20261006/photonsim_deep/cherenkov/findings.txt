larger than the sparse-table quadrature error, but small in absolute light.

Confirmed minor numerical defect: sparse refractive-index integration
  Water has ten phase-index knots from1.84 to4.51eV. Geant4 tabulates the
  cumulative integral of1/n(E)^2 by trapezoids at these knots, then linearly
  interpolates that cumulative integral at the beta-dependent threshold.
  A partial interval near threshold can consequently give a negative mean
  number of photons; PostStepDoIt emits zero when this happens.

  Minimal direct reproducer: mean_yield.cc builds the actual pinned water,
  calls installed G4Cerenkov::BuildPhysicsTable and GetAverageNumberOfPhotons.
  At beta=.734, G4 gives0.00659140583 photons/mm while the exact positive-part
  Frank–Tamm integral for precisely the same n(E) is0.02596135309 photons/mm.
  beta.73165–.73370 is physically above threshold (true beta_min.73164129)
  but G4 produces no photons. This is a local near-threshold error, not a
  detector-wide75% yield deficit.

  Analytic oracle: for a linearly varying n(E), the integral of1/n(E)^2 over
  a retained interval [Ea,Eb] is(Eb−Ea)/(na*nb). Split at each original knot
  and n=1/beta, integrate max(0,1−1/(beta*n)^2), multiply369.81/(eV cm).
  This isolates G4 numerical integration from the physical refractive-index
  model. Densifying the original linear n(E), without changing its shape,
  agrees with that oracle. A separate dense Daimon–Masumura Sellmeier table
  tests the physical knot provenance and is not used as the numerical fix.

Whole-track impact (job39971407, milano sdfmilan271)
  Actual pinned PhotonSim runs were observed before output storage. The
  observer records real G4 step lengths, both endpoint betas, photon births,
  and true emitting track. It avoids the independent production segment-
  ownership defect. All charged primary and secondary tracks are included;
  primary-track summaries are also saved. Exact/coarse integrals below use
  the SAME realized charged trajectories, avoiding shower-fluctuation bias.

  Total raw-light / production-QE-weighted fractional loss, respectively:
    e−0.30MeV:0.0843% /0.0414%
    e−0.50MeV:0.00614% /0.00383%
    e−10MeV:  0.000854% /0.000798%
    mu−1GeV: 0.000936% /0.000859%
  The primary500MeV proton loses0.331% of its own raw Cherenkov light;
  primary60MeV mu− loses0.119%. Other products dilute or alter event totals.
  The0.25MeV electron loses about9% of just0.00568 photons/event; expected
  production-QE weight before geometry is only3.46e−5/event. Its missing
  QE-weighted light is approximately3e−6/event, not a large detector error.

  Production QE weighting clips wavelength to[300,648.22]nm BEFORE applying
  SK_QE, following actual evaluate_optical_model behavior. It includes the
  accepted ultraviolet QE-clamp approximation. Geometry, absorption and
  electronics are deliberately absent from these emission-side weights.

Minimal sparse-table fix suggestion
  Densify the water RINDEX table while preserving its existing piecewise-
  linear function, and add threshold/whole-track convergence checks. The
  audit dense table uses1000 subdivisions per original interval; this is a
  verification choice, not a claimed optimal production size. Densification
  makes the artifact arbitrarily small; exact elimination requires a G4
  integral that integrates the partially retained interval consistently.
  No production files were changed.

Confirmed low-energy step-size dependence
  The default maximum proposed beta change is10% per step. G4Cerenkov uses
  the mean of pre/post-step beta for its total rate and photon spectrum,
  then independently samples a position weighted by endpoint rates.
  This does not integrate the continuous energy loss through threshold.

  The isolated dense-index build removes the sparse-table error while
  retaining precisely the same n(E). For10000 stopping0.3MeV electrons:

    max beta change     sampled photons/event     expected photons/event
    10% (default)       0.8881 +/-0.00982          0.891640 +/-0.002536
    0.1%                0.9863 +/-0.01018          0.981690 +/-0.002332
    0.01%               1.0095 +/-0.01027          0.984714 +/-0.002313

  Standard errors are from per-event variance. Expected values sum the exact
  n(E) Frank–Tamm mean over each actual step; they reduce photon-sampling
  noise, but do not assert those default coarse steps are physically exact.
  The0.1% and0.01% settings agree statistically. Relative to the finer run,
  the default expected rate is9.45% lower; production-QE-weighted expectation
  is0.091440 versus0.102326,10.64% lower. This is only0.093 missing emitted
  photons, or0.0109 incident-QE-weighted photons, per0.3MeV electron before
  geometry and water losses. No comparable large normal-event bias is
  established. Tests at1/10MeV and the muon/proton settings were consistent
  within their ensemble precision; the small ensembles cannot certify a
  sub-percent bound on all events.

  At default steps, about0.99% of sampled0.3MeV photons lie below threshold
  at their assigned position IF beta is assumed linear between endpoints.
  That conditional-path inconsistency disappears in the sampled0.01% run.
  This is a diagnostic; endpoints alone do not establish the true trajectory.
  Hard endpoint interactions, continuous loss, multiple scattering and
  beta's nonlinear dependence on kinetic energy cannot be uniquely separated
  from this observer alone. The convergence finding does not depend on that
  linear-path assumption.

  Minimal fix suggestion, before initialization:
    /process/optical/cerenkov/setMaxBetaChange 0.1
  Equivalent PhotonSim initialization:
    #include "G4OpticalParameters.hh"
    G4OpticalParameters::Instance()->SetCerenkovMaxBetaChange(0.1);
  The API value is percent. This is a demonstrated convergence setting for
  these inputs, not a universal proof of exact physics. Check runtime impact
  and broader production spectra before adopting it globally.

Checks passed and scope limits
  Actual material is H2O at1g/cm3 and293.15K, mean excitation energy78eV.
  All ten phase-index values agree with Daimon–Masumura20C Sellmeier values
  within5.5e−6. This verifies the cited model, not equivalence to actual SK
  water temperature/composition. Source material calibration is still needed
  before claiming exact real-detector agreement.
  For beta>=0.8, replacing the original linear n(E) with the dense physical
  Sellmeier fit changes the rate by at most0.0397% in the scanned range.
  G4 defaults are100 mean photons/step and10% maximum proposed beta change.
  Individual observed photons obey cos(theta)=1/(beta_mid*n(E)) to floating
  precision, and their birth times match the G4 step formula. These verify
  what G4 implements; local energy-angle-position consistency during a
  slowing step is addressed by the convergence result above.
  An independent exact conditional Frank–Tamm CDF test also passes for all
  five saved photon samples:0.3MeV at three step settings,100MeV electrons,
  and1GeV muons. KS p-values range0.146–0.890 across8881–200000 photons.
  This checks spectrum and its dispersion relationship with the sampled
  angle, conditional on G4's step-mean beta. It does not vindicate that
  step-mean approximation or test detector transport. See spectrum_results.json
  and job39972392 (milano/sdfmilan270).
  Endpoint discrete energy losses can enter G4's midpoint beta because
  Cerenkov PostStep order1000 follows ionisation2/brems3/annihilation5.
  This is a source-level mechanism, not yet a quantified production bug.
